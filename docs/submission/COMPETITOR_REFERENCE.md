## Full Comparison Paragraph

While Dead Man's Ledger was already in development, Myles published Dependency Day, another Independence Engines project built around a branching node graph. I loved seeing the same broad visual grammar arrive through a completely different idea.

Dependency Day asks what dependencies you cut.

Dead Man's Ledger asks what risks you take to keep currency moving.

His graph models severance. This one models circulation.

## Independent-Conception Note

Dead Man's Ledger was independently conceived as a frontier currency-route board game before Dependency Day was published. The public reference is included because both projects use graph language to explore the same competition theme from different directions.

## Distinct Mechanics

Dependency Day treats independence as severance: a branching network of dependencies that must be cut. Dead Man's Ledger treats independence as circulation: a route network that must stay connected enough for paper money, payroll, bank drafts, and public trust to keep moving.

## Same Graph, Different Engine

While Dead Man's Ledger was already in development, Myles published Dependency Day, another Independence Engines project built around a branching node graph. I loved seeing the same broad visual grammar arrive through a completely different idea.

Dependency Day treats independence as a network of dependencies that must be cut. Dead Man's Ledger treats independence as an economy that must remain connected.

His game asks: What do you cut?

Mine asks: What do you risk to keep the money moving?

His graph models severance. Mine models circulation. Seeing his finished implementation helped me sharpen that distinction and made me push harder on the route network as the literal engine of the game.
